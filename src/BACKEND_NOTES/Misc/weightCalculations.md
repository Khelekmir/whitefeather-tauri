Several alternate formulas exist to estimate a "healthy" body weight for men and women at a given height, beyond using the Body Mass Index (BMI). These formulas take into account factors like gender and frame size. Below are some of the most common ones:

### 1. **Devine Formula**
The Devine formula was originally created to estimate ideal body weight for medical use, particularly for drug dosing.

- **For Men**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 50 + 2.3 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

- **For Women**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 45.5 + 2.3 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

### 2. **Hamwi Formula**
The Hamwi formula is commonly used in clinical settings to estimate a "healthy" or "ideal" body weight.

- **For Men**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 48.0 + 2.7 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

- **For Women**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 45.5 + 2.2 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

### 3. **Miller Formula**
The Miller formula is another simple height-based estimate for ideal body weight, with slight variations between men and women.

- **For Men**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 56.2 + 1.41 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

- **For Women**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 53.1 + 1.36 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

### 4. **Robinson Formula**
The Robinson formula is similar to the Miller formula but provides slightly different coefficients.

- **For Men**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 52 + 1.9 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

- **For Women**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 49 + 1.7 \times (\text{height (in)} - 60)
  \]

### 5. **Broca’s Index**
Broca’s index is one of the oldest formulas, developed by the French surgeon Paul Broca in the 19th century. It is very simplistic.

- **For Men**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = \text{height (cm)} - 100
  \]

- **For Women**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = \text{height (cm)} - 105
  \]

### 6. **Lemmens Formula**
The Lemmens formula is considered more conservative and often used in bariatric medicine for weight management.

- **For Both Men and Women**:  
  \[
  \text{Ideal Body Weight (kg)} = 22 \times \text{height (m)}^2
  \]

This is essentially using a BMI of 22 as the reference for a healthy weight.

### Frame Size Adjustment
Some of these formulas (like Devine or Hamwi) assume an average body frame size, but frame size can affect healthy weight. To adjust based on frame size:
- **Large frame**: Add 10% to the calculated weight.
- **Small frame**: Subtract 10% from the calculated weight.

### Conclusion
Each of these formulas provides a rough estimate of a "healthy" body weight based on height and gender. The "ideal" weight can vary depending on individual factors like frame size, muscle mass, and overall health, so these formulas should be viewed as guidelines rather than strict rules.
